Professional Development / Reflection 04

Different voices.
One shared direction.

My reflection on collaboration, group development and the lessons that teamwork can teach me about communication, accountability and professional growth.

Rethabile Menyatso Business Intelligence & Data Analytics 2026

The bigger picture

Learning to work beyond myself.

As a first-year student in Business Intelligence and Data Analytics, I am learning that success is not only about individual ability. It also depends on how well I communicate, share responsibility and work with people whose perspectives may differ from my own.

I see group work as an opportunity to practise these skills before entering the professional environment. In a field where business decisions often depend on the combined knowledge of analysts, managers and other team members, collaboration is an important part of producing useful results.

The five stages below help me examine group development as a process. They also encourage me to consider what I can contribute, what I still need to improve and how I can become a more dependable team member.

The group journey

Five stages of growth.

Tuckman's model provides a framework for understanding how groups can move from initial introductions towards effective collaboration and eventual closure.

01
Getting started

Forming — Finding our place

At the beginning of group work, members are still getting to know one another and understanding what the task requires. I recognise that this stage can bring a mixture of curiosity, uncertainty and anticipation because everyone is trying to understand how the group will operate.

I would approach this stage by listening carefully, introducing my ideas respectfully and making an effort to understand the expectations of the task. Since I value collaboration and am willing to learn, I can use this period to identify how my strengths may support the group.

My lesson: A good start requires openness, clear expectations and a willingness to learn from people with different perspectives.

02
Working through differences

Storming — Handling challenges

As group members begin contributing more actively, differences in opinions, working styles or expectations may become more noticeable. I understand that disagreement does not automatically mean a group is failing. Sometimes it reveals that members care about the quality of the work but approach the task differently.

My responsibility is to avoid treating every different opinion as a personal disagreement. I can ask questions, explain my reasoning calmly and remain willing to reconsider an idea when another approach is stronger. If a challenge arises, I would encourage the group to return to the task requirements and agree on a practical way forward.

My lesson: Professionalism means responding to differences constructively instead of allowing them to interfere with the shared goal.

03
Building consistency

Norming — Creating a rhythm

The norming stage is about developing shared expectations. A group can become more organised when members understand their responsibilities, communicate regularly and agree on how work should be completed.

I can contribute by being dependable, respecting agreed deadlines and communicating when I need clarification. I also recognise the value of checking that everyone understands the plan rather than assuming that silence means agreement. Clear responsibilities can help reduce confusion and make the workload more manageable.

My lesson: Teamwork improves when expectations are discussed openly and members take responsibility for their contributions.

04
Working as one

Performing — Turning plans into results

During the performing stage, a group is better positioned to focus on the task because members understand their roles and can coordinate their efforts. This stage reminds me that collaboration should produce more than a collection of separate contributions: the work needs to form a clear and consistent final outcome.

In a Business Intelligence and Data Analytics context, this could mean combining research, checking the accuracy of information, organising data and presenting findings in a way that makes sense to the intended audience. I can contribute through attention to detail, problem solving and my developing digital skills, while remaining open to feedback from others.

My lesson: Effective teamwork combines individual strengths with shared responsibility for the quality of the final result.

05
Reflecting and moving forward

Adjourning — Recognising the learning

Adjourning occurs when the group completes its task and prepares to move on. I see this stage as an opportunity to evaluate not only what the group produced, but also what I learned about myself as a collaborator.

I would consider which contributions were effective, whether communication could have been clearer and what I would do differently in a future project. Recognising the effort of other members is also important because a completed task usually reflects several contributions rather than one person's work alone.

My lesson: Completing a task is valuable, but reflecting on the process helps me carry better habits into the next team experience.

I want to become the kind of team member who contributes ideas, respects people and takes responsibility for results.

My developing professional standard

What I am taking forward

Lessons beyond the assignment.

01

Communication

I want to express my ideas clearly, listen without interrupting and ask for clarification whenever expectations are uncertain.

02

Accountability

I need to take ownership of my responsibilities, respect agreed deadlines and communicate honestly about my progress.

03

Adaptability

I can become a stronger collaborator by accepting constructive feedback, adjusting my approach and recognising the value of other people's ideas.

My final reflection

Better together, better prepared.

Reflecting on group development has helped me recognise that collaboration involves more than dividing a task into smaller parts. It requires communication, trust, patience and a shared understanding of what the group is trying to achieve. Different stages may bring different demands, and I need to be prepared to learn from each one.

As I continue studying Business Intelligence and Data Analytics, I want to strengthen my ability to work with others, contribute thoughtfully and approach challenges with a problem-solving mindset. These habits will be useful in academic projects and in a future workplace where people need to combine their skills to make informed decisions.

My goal is not to assume that every group experience will be perfect. It is to become more aware of how I participate, how I respond when things are difficult and how I can help create a respectful and productive working environment.
